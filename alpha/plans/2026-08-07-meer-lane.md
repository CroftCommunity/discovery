## Problem statement

The **meer** — Drystone's blind store-and-forward node (Part 2 §6.6.2, D-meer) — is the single gating
unbuilt component for "the conversation stays alive while you sleep." The relay substrate is proven and
live; the meer is characterized but has no binary. Until it exists, "always-on" is a design claim, not
a capability.

Two things had blocked it, and this lane exists because both turned out to be misframings:

1. **"Find an MLS delivery service to adapt."** There isn't one, and the absence is structural. RFC 9750
   deliberately leaves the DS unspecified, so every shipped DS is bespoke to its product — and in
   practice a DS *is* the server half of a messenger (identity, group creation, KeyPackage hosting,
   Welcome distribution, room policy, ordering). OpenMLS ships a proof-of-concept; Phoenix R&D ships a
   real one inside a whole AGPL-3.0 application; MIMI standardized the role by putting an ordering hub
   in the middle, which is precisely what Drystone declines. **Everyone else's DS is their product, and
   we are building only the part that is not.**

2. **"The meer needs a store."** It doesn't. CISS is live, holds content-addressed bytes, and
   re-verifies them on read. Scoping the meer as its own service imported a storage design, a backup
   design, a durability promise, and an entrenchment problem — none of which it needs.

Phase 10 (`plans/croft-stack/10-drystone-layer.md`) gates the *history-convergence server* on the fold
and MLS becoming real. That gate binds the history store. **It does not bind the meer**: because the
per-author index lives inside the seal, the meer cannot order and does not need to, so it needs zero
fold knowledge. The meer is unblocked today.

## Approach

### The shape

**CISS grows a typed-chain substrate; the meer is a thin gateway service over it.**

```
   CISS — the typed-chain substrate                    ← the reusable thing
   ├─ chain kinds (queue / file-sync / history-convergence), genesis-fixed,
   │    declared in the OWNER's manifest slot, bound into the signed preimage
   ├─ custodial write, gated to the queue kind ONLY, failing closed on unknown
   ├─ owner-declared per-slot ceilings
   └─ per-chain metering, transit and at-rest

   gateway services — thin, separate processes         ← the disposable things
   ├─ meer            iroh ALPN → deposit / drain
   ├─ history store   G-hist → envelope / reconcile   (gated separately)
   └─ (file, chat, … as they arrive)
```

The meer itself is five operations: accept a publish; `PUT` the blob once to CISS; append an entry to
each recipient's queue chain; serve a drain (have/want diff, hand back blobs, ack, prune); sweep
expired and leave a watermark. No ordering, no group state, no key handling, no storage layer, no
backup story.

Deposit is pub/sub (one publish, many recipients); drain is a mailbox (each recipient, own pace, own
window). The two halves carry the two distinct auth questions — deposit gated at the meer's admission
policy, drain at CISS account identity.

### The lane

| Phase | What | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Spike** — does the shape hold against real MLS traffic? | **Now.** Runs against plain CISS as it exists; no substrate needed |
| 1 | CISS typed-chain substrate: kinds, custodial write, ceilings | Phase 0 green + a security-review pass |
| 2 | Meer gateway v0 against the real substrate | Phase 1 |
| 3 | croft-stack deploy: queue-mode tenant, UDP exception, netns | Phase 2 |
| 4 | Metering surfaced: offline-data fraction, bounded retention | Phase 3 |
| 5 | Custody dial: pooled/meer-owned mode for bootstrap and scale | Product need |

Phase 0 is deliberately a **discovery phase**. Its job is not to prove the design right but to tell us
the shape of the things currently sketched — which is why the spike spec carries eight shape-learning
scenarios alongside its two must-pass claims, and why it names in advance what would falsify the
design.

### Why Phase 0 runs against plain CISS

The substrate (Phase 1) is the piece that carries the security-review cost — it is the first time a
non-owner can cause a write in an owner's namespace. Building it before the spike would mean guessing
at what it must support. Running the spike first, with the missing pieces registered as `SPEC-DELTA`
stand-ins, means Phase 1 is informed rather than speculative.

## Reasoning

**Why per-DID queues are the default.** The anti-entrenchment guard in `meer-superpeer-design.md` is
state portability — "a meer that becomes the sole custodian of recoverable state is the entrenchment
failure." Per-DID queues do not satisfy that guard, they **retire** it: there is nothing to port
because the mail never left home. Portability is a promise that a helper will give your data back;
ownership is not having handed it over. Stated for a user: *the always-on helper that catches your
messages while you sleep never holds them.* That is a materially different claim from every
self-hosted or federated messenger.

**Why custody is a dial and not a fixed choice.** Per-DID buys ownership and legible accounting;
meer-owned pooled queues buy a meer that knows less about you, plus a bootstrap path for someone with
no account and cheaper operation for idle-heavy groups. The trade cannot be maximised on both sides,
so it belongs alongside the existing confidentiality dial (Tier 0/1/2/no-mirror) rather than being
decided once for everyone.

**Why kind-gating rather than per-write authorization.** A type check on an owner-signed,
genesis-fixed field is a structural gate; a grant lookup is a policy gate that can be misconfigured.
It also bounds the security review to "can a non-queue chain ever be written custodially," which is
tractable, rather than "is every custodial write path correctly authorized," which is not.

**Why metering is separate from billing.** The transit meter *is* the **offline-data fraction** — the
number `meer-superpeer-design.md` names as the only thing that sizes a meer fleet and lists as
unmeasured. It falls out of the accounting rather than needing instrumentation. Keeping it unbilled
also resolves the abuse case: because mail lands in the recipient's namespace, spam would otherwise
cost the victim money; unbilled, the owner-declared ceiling does the defending and the meter only
watches.

**Why the gateway pattern needs custodian mode to work at all.** A shaping service acting as itself
would bill everything to its own account. Acting as custodian, bytes meter to the person they belong
to. The mechanism invented to hold mail is what lets any future CISS-plus-whatever front the store
honestly — which is the generalisation this lane is really about.

## Open items

Carried out of the design session, to be resolved by Phase 0 or by decision:

- **Who mints the custodian grant** — per-persona enrolment out of band (lean: matches Part 2 §5.4,
  "a persona's *use* of a meer is a per-persona decision") versus riding Group governance so a Group
  can point its members at a meer collectively.
- **Meter retention policy** — aggregate shape and window. The gap named in session: *the mail purges
  at 14 days, the meter does not, unless we say so*, which would leave the meer's most sensitive
  artifact as the only thing that never expires.
- **`Welcome`/`GroupInfo` versus the 2 MiB `MAX_OBJECT_BYTES` cap.** The cap is load-bearing (it came
  from a real memory-exhaustion finding in the 2026-08-03 security review), and `GroupInfo` with the
  embedded ratchet tree is O(N). Three options — out of scope for v0, transparent chunking, or ship
  the tree out of band per Part 2 §6.9.1 — and **the measurement (spike S8) picks between them**.
  Option 3 may show the cap is re-discovering, from the storage side, a constraint the spec already
  resolved from the protocol side.
- **Custody-dial default per Group**, and whether bootstrap always implies the pooled mode.
- **Deliver-once versus race** when no device group is present — the spike's S4 makes the dependency
  concrete.

## Honest limits (recorded so they are not rediscovered as surprises)

- A member can validate everything the meer **asserts** — the chain is hash-linked, entries are signed,
  and the mailbag is checkable against what was drained, so over-reporting, under-delivery, and
  reordering are caught. A member **cannot detect withholding**: something handed to the meer that
  never appears. That is undetectable by construction, and is why D-peer corroboration exists as an
  independent source.
- The kind gate bounds bleed-over *between* chain kinds. It says nothing about a custodian misbehaving
  *within* a queue — that is what the ceiling, revocation, and the untrusted-by-assumption posture are
  for.
- Custodian mode necessarily erodes CISS's "the server never interprets a head" property. The erosion
  is bounded to reading one owner-signed field at the dispatch choke point, and is named rather than
  smoothed over.
